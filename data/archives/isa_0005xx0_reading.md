## 1. THE WARD QUESTION — answered, twice over

This was the priority. The file contains **two** documents that lay out the hospital's
internal composition, and together they close the bed accounting completely.

### 1a. The founding memorandum, 1935 — bed allocation by section

**ISA pp. 323–331.** Director of Medical Services to Chief Secretary, ref. 108/107,
replying to Chief Secretary's M/47/34 of 4 September 1935. Subject "Proposed Government
Hospital at Haifa". Transcribed from the page images at 250 dpi.

Paragraph 2 (**p.323**, verified against the image):

> "The bedstrength of the Government Hospital, Haifa, at present is 130 of which 40 beds are
> for fever and isolation cases, including typhoid fever and dysentery. Due to the increased
> number of cases requiring hospital accommodation, the Secretary of State has recently
> approved of the increase of the bedstrength to 150 by the construction of a temporary hut
> designed to accommodate 20 cases."

Paragraph 5 (**p.327**, verified against the image) — the **General Section**:

| Section | Beds |
|---|---|
| Admission Section | 4 |
| General Surgical cases | 60 |
| General Medical cases | 60 |
| Maternity Section | 24 |
| Maternity Isolation Section | 3 |
| Gynaecological Section | 10 |
| General Isolation Section | 4 |
| British Section | 24 |
| **Total** | **189** |

Paragraph 5(b), foot of **p.327** — the **Fever Section**, "built on the pavilion system but
due to the fact that the area is very much restricted, it is proposed to build pavilions of
two storeys". Total proposed fever accommodation **88 beds**:

| | Ground | Upper |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Isolation Pavilion | 22 | 22 |
| 1 Isolation Pavilion | 14 | 14 |
| 1 Observation Pavilion (p.329) | 8 | 8 |

**p.329:** "The total bedstrength of the whole Hospital will consequently be **277 beds**."
189 + 88 = 277. The arithmetic closes.

**A crucial exclusion**, same paragraph (p.327, verified):

> "It will not provide for such highly infectious cases as Plague, Cholera, Yellow Fever,
> Smallpox and Typhus for which, it is proposed, accommodation will be provided elsewhere in
> the near future, under conditions that will comply with the International Sanitary
> Convention."

So the Bat Galim hospital was designed *without* provision for the five quarantinable
diseases. This is direct evidence for [[project_haifa_lazaret]]: a separate establishment for
those diseases was Government policy in 1935, and see §4 below for what happened in 1942.

Paragraph 7 (p.329) sites the hospital "near the base of the harbour breakwater… between that
and the eastern boundary of the property of the Carmelite Sisters", requiring purchase of a
small part of the convent property; paragraph 8, "51 donums of which 11.929 donums are State
Domain", with the P.W.D. architects already objecting that **the site is too small**.

Note that V here (44 + 16 + 14 = 74 beds) **differs from the 1935 memorandum's 88**: the
revised 1936 schedule replaced the second 14+14 isolation pavilion and the 8+8 observation
pavilion with a 16-bed single-room pavilion and a 14-bed single-storey pavilion. The
Isolation bed strength actually reported in 1940 was 76 and in 1941–44 was 98 — see
`isa_readings.md`, the Table (a) series — so the built Isolation establishment tracks the
1936 schedule's 74 closely at first and then grows well past it.

**What this gives the register work.** The register's ward names can now be matched against a
designed unit list: Male Medical, Female Medical, Male Surgical, Female Surgical (26 beds
each), British, Maternity, Maternity Isolation, Gynaecological, Fever/Isolation pavilions,
plus Out-Patients/Admission. In particular the schedule confirms a **distinct Gynaecological
Section of 10 beds existed in the design**, which bears on [[project_ward_gynecology_error]] —
the ward existed, it was simply far too small to account for 2,190 records, so the "Gen."
misreading hypothesis stands and is now positively supported: a 10-bed unit cannot generate
that volume.

## 2. THE OPENING — occupation and handover, precisely dated

Three independent statements, all from the file:

**ISA p.532** — Director of Medical Services to Director of Public Works, ref. 123/61, P.W.D.
registry stamp **11 JAN 1939**, subject "Water supplies to Government Hospital, Haifa":

> "(i) Occupation of the Hospital buildings started in the second half of September, 1938."

**ISA p.537** — Director of Medical Services to Director of Public Works, ref. 108/107 R,
stamped **6 JAN 1939**, subject "Maintenance of New Government Hospital, Haifa" (verified
against the page image):

> "2. The official taking over took place for the main blocks and Pavilions A, B, C, D
> **October 3rd, 1938**. For Pavilions E and F **December 21st, 1938**.
> The guarantee period will expire twelve months after acceptance, i.e. for the main blocks
> and Pavilions A, B, C, D October 3rd, 1939, For Pavilions E and F December 21st, 1939."

**ISA p.510** — Director of Public Works to District Engineer, Haifa, 28 February 1939, asks
what water supplied "between the 1st of June and the 3rd of October, 1938, to the new
Government Hospital" was used for, and about the allocation "for the old Hospital at Haifa …
**up to the time it was evacuated**".

**This resolves the December 1938 question in [[project_hospital_two_buildings]].** The
sequence was: occupation began in the second half of **September 1938**; the main blocks and
Pavilions A–D were formally taken over from the contractor on **3 October 1938**; Pavilions E
and F followed on **21 December 1938**. The December event was therefore not merely a
ceremony — it was a real, dated handover of two pavilions. The NB20→NB21 register seam at
October 1938 sits exactly on the main-blocks handover.

**No ceremony material in the file.** Sweeps for "ceremony", "inaugurat", "official opening"
and "December 1938" return **zero pages**. This is a P.W.D. works file; the ceremonial side of
the opening, and any guest list, is not here. A well-evidenced negative.

A later handover appears too: **ISA p.1055**, 6 March 1940, "Pavilions 'E' and 'F' taking
over" — a snagging list between the Steward and the contractor, mostly missing plugs and
chains, two broken porcelain shower handles, a cracked wash basin, two serving hatches whose
"wood work has cracked and come away from the hinges" after the rains. Signed **(Sgd.)
Pettitt, STEWARD**, and citing **Dr. Macqueen** and the Matron.

## 3. THE OLD HOSPITAL — the St. Luke's lease, and why the new hospital was built

The single most consequential incidental finding, and it settles an open question in
[[project_stlukes_prebatgalim]].

**ISA p.323**, the 1935 founding memorandum, paragraph 1 (verified against the image):

> "The very rapid increase in the population of Haifa and surroundings during the past few
> years has created a demand for hospital accommodation which the present Government Hospital
> there can no longer meet. This taken in conjunction with the fact that **the present leased
> premises of St. Luke's Mission Hospital must be vacated by the end of December, 1938**,
> makes it imperative that proposals for the building of a Government Hospital in Haifa
> should receive very early consideration."

So: **the Government Hospital was, in 1935, operating in leased St. Luke's Mission Hospital
premises**, on a lease expiring end of December 1938. That is the pre-Bat-Galim building. The
gap that `project_stlukes_prebatgalim` recorded as unevidenced — St. Luke's mission hospital
closed March 1929, Bat Galim complete January 1938 — is now bridged: the Government took the
St. Luke's premises on lease and ran its hospital there. The lease expiry date also explains
the whole timetable: the new hospital *had* to be occupied before the end of 1938, and it was,
by three months.

The old hospital's establishment in 1935 was **130 beds, of which 40 fever and isolation**,
rising to 150 with a temporary 20-bed hut approved by the Secretary of State. Compare the new
building's 277 designed beds — slightly under double.

Paragraph 2 of the memorandum (**p.325**) also records the pressure on the old building:

> "…at present beds which should be allotted to general medical and surgical cases, have to be
> given up for the accommodation of cases of Typhoid Fever. This situation now arises annually
> without any epidemic prevalence of this or any other disease."

And paragraph 3, the second driver: "the increase of British Officials, Police and Residents
for the hospitalisation of whom no adequate facilities exist", plus an undertaking "to provide
accommodation for emergency and fever cases for the Military establishment in Haifa", with
married quarters for the Haifa regiment expected to increase that demand further. This is the
origin of the **British Section** and its 24 beds.

Beyond this the file says almost nothing about the old building. Only three pages in 1,569
mention it at all (pp. 323, 332, 510). **Where the old hospital stood is not stated anywhere in
this file.** For that, `0010qhu` ("Old Haifa Hospital & Main Disinfecting Station", 174 pp,
unopened) remains the place to look.

## 4. THE PLAGUE UNIT, 1941–42 — a separate building beside the hospital

A distinct, datable episode, and directly relevant to [[project_haifa_lazaret]] and
[[project_diphtheria_isolation_reconcile]].

Recall from §1a that the 1935 design **deliberately excluded** plague, cholera, yellow fever,
smallpox and typhus. In late 1941 that gap was closed by building a separate unit on the site.

- **ISA p.1301** (oversized sheet), P.W.D. minute headed **"Haifa Hosp. Plague Unit"**,
  December 1941: "Met DDMS at his office 16th inst. Lengthy discussion, resulting in minor
  adjustments of requirements as shewn in DMS plans and DDMS agreement to our proposing
  different planning arrangements. **Schedule of accommodation remains substantially the
  same.**" Minuted onward 19.12.41 and 23.12.41, referring to Dr. MacQueen's letter of
  18.12.41.
- **ISA p.1347**, Director of Public Works, **22 January 1942**: confirms that "Mr. Winter
  handed to Dr. Macqueen on 22.1.1942 for retention a **sketch plan of the building for the
  proposed Plague Unit**", with a siting suggestion on a site plan borrowed from Mr. Colburn.
- **ISA p.1380**, "Abstract of Estimate No. **HA/204**", 1942 — priced bill of quantities for
  the Plague Unit (R.C.C. foundations, plinth walls, foundation beams, outside walls,
  partitions at 12 cm and 6 cm).
- **ISA p.1388**, **9 March 1942**, subject "**Plague Service Station Haifa Hosp.**":
  "The estimate for the abovementioned is I understand £P.2,700. I send herewith F.A. for
  £P.1700."
- **ISA p.1411**, P.W.D. Jerusalem to District Engineer, **16 March 1942**: instructs that
  the walls be built "in concrete or burnt bricks in substitution of the poured concrete
  hitherto required", reducing timber requirements for shuttering; and — the siting detail —
  "to facilitate the difficulties of foundations and expansion joint of **the adjacent
  existing verandah** the new building may be moved 90 cms away from the edge of verandah and
  30 cms or so to the south of the position shewn on the plan."
- **ISA p.1412**, **18 March 1942**: asks the D.M.S. to sign and return **Drawing No. B/1641**.
- **ISA p.1421**, **1 April 1942**: Financial Allocation No. 1990 of 31.3.42 for expenditure
  to 31 March 1942.

**What this establishes.** The plague unit was designed between December 1941 and March 1942,
cost about **£P.2,700**, was a small masonry building **built hard against an existing
verandah of the hospital**, and is drawing **B/1641**. It is called both "Plague Unit" and
"Plague Service Station". This dates and localises the 1942 plague department precisely, and
confirms it was physically part of the hospital site rather than a separate lazaret — which
matters for reading "Isolation Haifa" vs "Govt. Hosp." in the 1942–44 nominal returns
(`000zbri`).

## 6. THE TENDER, THE COST, AND THE LABOUR DISPUTE

### 6a. The tender

**ISA p.11 — Agenda, Tenders Board Meeting, 27 May 1937, "Haifa Government Hospital".**
This is the adjudication itself. Agenda items included the results of resubmitted tenders
including Customs; a communication from a **Mr. V. Kohn** regarding the **Bovis Ltd.** tender
and the Attorney General's opinion; a letter to **Solel Boneh Co.** regarding the Agent and
Engineer proposed for carrying out the contract if allotted; and:

> "5. Adjudication of Tenders
> (a) General tender excluding Prime Cost Sums — 1. Sanitation. 2. Electric Installation and
> lights. 3. Lifts. 4. Built in Cupboards. 5. Rain water drainage. 6. Connection to [main]
> drain.
> **Solel Boneh Ltd. / Bovis Ltd. / Gut-Gurevitch.**
> (b) Sanitation tender — Herouth Cooperation. / T. Maude.
> 6. Conditions of contract and addition of clause reference employment of **50% Arab and 50%
> Jewish labour in terms of wages**."

The main contract went to **Solel Boneh**.

### 6b. The cost

**ISA p.18**, Tenders Board minutes: the Director of Medical Services explained the increase
in the estimate "on account of increased cost of materials since September 1936"; the Director
of Public Works recommended opening tenders and asking the two or three lowest to add customs
duties; a clause was inserted making the contractor liable for customs duty on all imported
materials. Then:

> "A preliminary estimate worked out on Solel Boneh['s] Price and found to be approximately
> **£P.110,200**. Mr. Kempinski was to check this figure and produce a definite figure to
> cable to [the] Secretary of State."

An earlier, smaller order of magnitude appears at **ISA p.332** in a 1935 costing: site
19 donums at £P.1,200 = £P.22,800; buildings £P.44,000; furniture & equipment £P.4,000
(additional to equipment at the existing hospital); special apparatus (kitchen, X-ray,
refrigerators, disinfecting plant etc.) £P.6,100. Item 1 estimated by the Department of Lands,
item 2 by the Director of Public Works. The gap between that £P.77k-ish 1935 sketch and the
£P.110,200 tender price of 1937 is the measure of how the project grew.

Financing runs through the file as **Special Warrants** (34 pages), e.g. **ISA p.1082**,
Special Warrant No. 5[?] of 1939/40, Head XXX Public Works Extraordinary, Sub-head No. 30
"Construction of Government Hospital, Haifa", Secretariat file M/47/36.

### 6c. The Arab labourers' petition, September 1937

**ISA pp. 727–729.** A petition to **His Excellency the Officer Administering the Government**,
through the **Northern District Commissioner**, on the letterhead of **I. A. Saadeh, Advocate,
Haifa**, dated **18 September 1937**, P.W.D. Central Registry stamp 16 SEP 1937 on the copy at
p.729.

The complaint (p.727):

> "The Government through the [Director] of the Public Works Department had … contracted with
> Messrs. Solel Boneh Co. for the [construction] of the Government Hospital, Haifa, under
> certain conditions stated therein. **One of these conditions binds the said company to
> employ a number of Arab labourers, who should receive half of [the] sum of the total wages
> paid to the whole labour.** … [Work on] the Building [has been] started exceeding two months
> [during] which the company did not employ the sufficient number of the Arab labourers **nor
> paid even one tenth of the wages paid to the Jewish labourers**."

The prayer (p.729) asks that Arab labourers "take an effective part in the work and that the
wages be equally paid to the Arab and Jewish labourer"; that "a neutral superintendent be
appointed to supervise the proper enforcement of the provisions of the contracts referring to
the Arab labourers"; that returns be furnished of the number of Arab and Jewish labourers and
the wages paid to each since work started, and if the totals are unequal, that more Arab
labourers be employed "until a comparative amount of wages is reached"; and that "the Solel
Boneh should not refuse any of the Arab labourers without reasonable cause".

Copied to the Director of Public Works. It records that repeated "interviews and written
protests had been made to the Northern District [Commissioner]" beforehand.

**I did not find a reply or any Government decision on this petition in the file.** Only two
pages in 1,569 carry the labour clause at all (727, 729). Whether the 50/50 condition was
enforced is not answerable from this file. Given the date — September 1937, in the middle of
the Arab Revolt — this is a substantial finding on its own terms: the building of the hospital
was itself contested ground, and the Mandate had written an ethnic labour-parity clause into
the contract that a Haifa advocate was alleging, within two months, had been ignored.

## 7. WARTIME: THE 1940 AIR RAID AND THE CASUALTY ENTRANCE

**ISA p.1121**, District Engineer to Director of Public Works, stamped **AUG 1940**, subject
"Government Hospital Haifa — Emergency Approach road":

> "During the last air raid on Haifa the Government Hospital was called upon to deal with a
> large number of casualties in a short time. It was found that with the existing approach
> road to the casualty entrance, serious congestion occurred, as it was not possible for an
> ambulance to leave the courtyard whilst casualties were being dealt with at the entrance.
> It is therefore urged that a second emergency approach & entrance be constructed in
> accordance with the attached sketch No. **HA/4171**."

**ISA p.1130**, Haifa, **13 August 1940**: the request came from the Health Authorities at the
hospital; the Senior Medical Officer approached the District Commissioner, who agreed to spend
**£P.26 from A.R.P. funds** on opening the gate. The S.M.O. thought the gate alone would
suffice; the District Engineer disagreed, arguing that without the roadway there would be "a
dust nuisance in an emergency" in summer and the gate would be "inaccessible from the hospital
side" in the wet season.

**A.R.P. measures at the hospital** were still being chased in 1943 (**ISA p.1515**, 4 May
1943, the Acting Director of Public Works impatiently: "The point is not who raised the
question or who is to elucidate the matter. What I desire to know is why the work is held
up"). A **Civil Defence Adviser's Office** letterhead appears at p.1560 (1942).

This is a direct lead for the register: the Italian air raids on Haifa in 1940 should be
visible as a casualty spike in the admissions data, and the hospital was demonstrably the
receiving point.

## 8. THE ROUTINE BULK — what the other ~1,400 pages are

Stated plainly so nobody sweeps it again. The great majority of the file is works
administration, and is one-line material:

- **Maintenance liability** (113 pages mention maintenance) — the long-running argument over
  where P.W.D.'s responsibility ended and the contractor's or the Department of Health's began
  during and after the defects liability period. ISA pp. 640–645 are representative.
- **Lifts** (69 pages) — installation by Nechushtan Ltd., then maintenance contracts running
  to the end of the Mandate (and continuing in the separate file `000v2kc`).
- **Electrical installation** (104 pages) — wiring, lighting, the emergency generator. ISA
  p.1435 is a good example: a 1943-ish District Engineer's memo costing a 16–17 kW alternator
  with 22 hp engine at £P.750 plus £P.550 installation = £P.1,100, versus a 10 kVA plant at
  £P.550, warning that with the smaller plant the lifts and sewage pumps could not run
  together and "lights will have to be greatly restricted".
- **Telephone and signalling** (96 pages) — the intercommunication telephone, light-signals
  system, clocks and bells.
- **Water supply** (68 pages) — including the 1939 consumption figures already noted, and the
  argument at pp. 510/532 over what water was used for during construction versus occupation.
- **Special Warrants, Financial Allocations, Abstracts of Estimate and vouchers** (~60 pages)
  — the money trail.
- **Gardens** (10 pages) — £P.205.902 for "garden's first installation and maintenance"
  (p.532).
- File covers, blank versos, minute slips, "ON HIS MAJESTY'S SERVICE" envelopes, and reused
  scrap (p.1302 is a Court "Order for Refund of Court Deposits" form used upside down as note
  paper).

## Method

The file has **no text layer** (`pdftotext` returns 1,569 bytes — one form feed per page), so
everything here rests on OCR plus image reading.

1. **Page-dimension scan** to find oversized sheets. Note: this file's page sizes are *noisy*
   (every page is a slightly different crop), so the "odd dimensions" trick described in
   `isa_readings.md` produces ~800 false positives here. Sorting by **page area** works: the
   genuinely oversized sheets are pp. 408, 1224, 1223, 407, 773, 2, 1, 1493 (750–855k pt²
   against a ~550k median). Of these, **p.2 and p.1493 were the two unchecked ones** and both
   turned out to be **file covers** (File No. 1 and Vol. 5) — useful for the bundle structure
   in the header table above, not substantive.
2. **Full-file OCR.** 1,569 pages rendered at 150 dpi and passed through Tesseract with
   `--psm 1`, which does its own orientation detection and handles the sideways and upside-down
   scans without manual rotation. 10-way parallel, ~25 minutes wall-clock on 12 cores. This is
   the step that made the file tractable, and it is worth doing for the other large unopened
   ISA files. A resumable per-page script pattern:
   ```
   pdftoppm -r 150 -png -f $p -l $p file.pdf out/w$p
   tesseract out/w$p-*.png ocr/$p --psm 1
   ```
   (skip if `ocr/$p.txt` already exists; drive with `seq 1 N | xargs -P 10`).
3. **Keyword triage** over the OCR text for each of the six priorities, then
4. **Targeted re-render at 220–250 dpi** for every page quoted above, autocontrasted
   (`PIL.ImageOps.autocontrast`, cutoff 1) because many pages are faint carbon copies, and
   read as images.

**Reliability.** Tesseract on these scans is good enough to *locate* and to read clean
typescript, but it is not reliable for figures. Every number quoted in §1a, §2 and §6b was
re-read from the page image at 250 dpi. Two OCR errors caught this way and corrected here:
the General Section total reads "169" in OCR but **189** on the page, and the old hospital's
bedstrength reads "150" in OCR but **130** on the page. Where I have not re-read a figure from
the image I have said so or left it out. Pages 281, 283, 285, 288, 324, 326, 328, 330 and
similar even-numbered pages in the schedule run are blank versos with bleed-through; the
bleed-through is legible when mirrored and is *not* separate content.

**Uncertain readings.** The Special Warrant number at p.1082 is either 5 or 58 (handwritten,
partly obscured). The exact date stamp on p.1121 is "AUG 1940" with the day illegible. The
Fever Section's "16 beds (single rooms)" at p.287 is clear, but its pavilion is described only
by ditto marks, so its storey count is inferred from the ditto to line (a), i.e. double storey.
