# Reading the Hebrew article corpus

Session E, 2026-08-26. A read — not a count — of the 349 articles in
`heb_reading_queue.tsv` where Haifa is the town nearest a hospital mention.
Queue built from `heb_art_qualified_union.tsv`; every id resolves at
`https://www.nli.org.il/en/newspapers/` via the Jrayed/NLI Veridian front door.

Entries are what the article *says*, with the reading marked. `[?]` means the
OCR or the context leaves it open; `[X]` means it turned out not to be Haifa's
Government Hospital and is recorded so the next reader does not re-open it.

## What the reading changed

Fourteen things this settles, narrows or opens, with the article that does it.
Everything else below is material for the essay and the timeline.

**Settled**

1. **The Bat Galim building has a full chronology.** Announced September 1935 at
   260 beds (`dhy19350912`); contracted to Solel Boneh at ~£90,000 with a 50/50
   Jewish-Arab employment clause, June 1937 (`haolam19370617`); ground broken at
   Bat Galim within days (`dav19370618`); **construction completed September
   1938** on the architect's assistant's own statement (`haretz19381223`);
   working, equipped from the old hospital, with 230 beds by **18 November 1938**
   (`hbkr19381118`); opened ceremonially 22 December 1938 at £120,000 and 225
   beds (`dav19381223`). The registers' October 1938 NB20→NB21 seam now sits
   inside an independently evidenced window.
2. **The architect was Erich Mendelsohn**, with Dr Kampinsky as his assistant —
   and Mendelsohn did not attend his own opening (`hbkr19381223`). Relevant to
   the Zawara/Mendelsohn correction in `[[project_cathedra_article]]`.
3. **A bed-occupancy denominator for 1938: 133.5 beds occupied on an average
   day**, from the Department of Health's own annual report, against Hadassah
   Haifa's 67.5 and Jaffa's 91 (`hbkr19400101`).
4. **The terminus is 2 May 1948**, when the hospital passed to Haifa
   municipality with 288 beds, found empty, four Arab bodies still in the
   mortuary (`haretz19480503`).
5. **The January 1948 walkout has a date and an inquiry.** All Jewish patients
   were moved out and the Jewish staff left on **31 December 1947**; doctors and
   clerks returned about 28 January 1948, the nurses did not
   (`hmf19480126`, `hbkr19480129`). This is the *Mitteilungsblatt* finding in
   `[[project_mb_press_findings]]`, with the Situation Committee's evidence.

**Narrowed**

6. **The isolation question.** The Public Works Department **built the plague
   department beside the Government Hospital in 1942** and added a 20-bed
   pavilion during the war (`haretz19450710`); the smallpox death of June 1944
   occurred "in the quarantine house beside the Government Hospital"
   (`hbkr19440601`); the October 1947 cholera scare was resolved in the
   hospital's own laboratory (`hmf19471008`). And the Yishuv's own January 1948
   statement: "**this hospital contains the only department for infectious
   diseases in the north of the country**" (`hmf19480126`). For 1942–48 the
   register plausibly *is* the denominator for plague and smallpox, which
   `[[project_haifa_lazaret]]` had left open. The Arabic الكرنتينا is a port
   quarantine station and a different institution.
7. **The 1944–45 plague is countable.** A running series with dates, totals and
   outcomes: 22 cases by 6 September 1944, **64 cases / 18 dead / 36 recovered /
   10 still in hospital since 20 June, as of 17 November 1944**, and **72 / 22 /
   47 / 3 by 5 January 1945**; over by August 1947. This revises the
   "cholera/plague/smallpox OPEN" caveat in
   `[[project_press_epidemic_leadlag]]` — here the press counts alongside the
   register rather than lagging it.
8. **Ard al-Yahud is attested by name and date**: two of three plague patients
   admitted in mid-October 1944 were Arab children from Ard al-Yahud, the third
   a Jewish watchman from the port (`hzh19441016`). Feeds
   `[[project_ard_harat_yahud]]`.

**Opened, and cautionary**

9. **The press's casualty names are the hospital's own bulletins.** Several
   items say so explicitly (`haretz19470701`, `hmf19470801`, `ahr19470209`).
   Press and register therefore share a source; agreement is not corroboration.
   And the names were not always right — after the June 1946 railway workshops
   raid "the CID stuck names on the bodies, but they think the names are
   inaccurate" (`hbkr19460619`).
10. **A judge found the hospital negligent**, twice within six weeks, for
    discharging poor patients before they had recovered, in a case where the
    death was assigned to the hospital rather than the assailant
    (`ahr19471106`). Bears on both mortality and discharge dates.
11. **Unidentified entries have documented causes.** Thirteen of the September
    1940 air-raid dead went unburied because unidentified, four never identified
    (`hashaah19400923`); two *ma'apilim* were buried as "**Abraham son of
    Abraham**" (`dav19470212`, `haretz19461206`); a headless body lay for days in
    January 1948 with its community undetermined (`haretz19480123`); and the
    mortuary advertised itself as an identification service by telephone
    (`dav19480114`).
12. **Bodies could leave the register the way they came.** In January 1947 some
    two hundred mourners climbed the fence, entered the hospital and removed a
    body over the wall (`ahr19470121`).
13. **Named staff across the whole period** — Dr Malchi (1931–45, ending as
    director), Dr Haddad, **Dr N. Hamzeh (1936–48, the last director, who left
    for Lebanon)**, Dr Y. Shapira, Dr McCowen (director to 1939, later Director
    of Medical Services), Colonel Heron, Dr Ibrahim Isa'is, Dr Mishlani, Dr
    Laufer, Dr Tahuian, Dr MacNeil. Enough for a staff table.
14. **Function beyond admissions.** The hospital was the district mortuary, the
    medico-legal examiner, the examining station for arriving *ma'apilim*, a
    prisoners' ward with its own cell, a nursing school, a maternity ward from
    which a baby was stolen, the terminus of two named bus routes, and — twice —
    the place where an abandoned newborn was brought in alive, and once dead.

## 1930–1935

**1931-01-21, Davar** (`dav19310121-01.2.5`) — A storm across Haifa and Tel
Aviv. "At the fence of the Government Hospital the labourer Abd Allah Mustafa
was killed by a live wire that had come down." The same paragraph reports the
sea wall at Bat Galim destroyed and the bath-houses between Bat Galim and the
prison camp levelled. Locates the hospital seven years before the move, and
incidentally dates the Bat Galim sea wall as pre-existing.

**1931-03-01, Doar HaYom** (`dhy19310301-01.2.41`) — Murder trial of men of
Daliyat al-Karmel before the Haifa district court. Among the witnesses, "a
clerk of the Government Hospital and the nurse who treated the wounded woman."
A named admission ought to sit behind this. **Register lead.**

**1931-03-08, Doar HaYom** (`dhy19310308-01.2.33`) — Haifa municipal council
resolves "to allocate £100 towards paving part of the road beside the
Government Hospital, as the Government requires." Mountain Road, and the
Government pressing the municipality to pay for its approach.

**1931-10-08, Davar** (`dav19311008-01.2.37`) — "Dr Malchi has been transferred
from Safed to the Government Hospital in Haifa." Staff movement.

**1932-06-23, Doar HaYom** (`dhy19320623-01.2.33`) — Under the heading "The
arrangements of the Government Hospital": relatives of patients "complain
bitterly that their sick lie in high fever for want of ice in the said
hospital," and that the burden of fetching ice from town falls on the family.
"Is such an arrangement to be the practice in a *government* hospital?" One of
the few pieces here on conditions inside the wards rather than on admissions.

**1933-01-18, Davar** (`dav19330118-01.2.43`) — Department of Health
retrenchment: Beersheba hospital closed, the new infectious-diseases hospital
postponed, Hadassah Haifa's surgical department shut for want of donations.
Context for what the Government Hospital was absorbing.

**1933-07-03, Haaretz** (`haretz19330703-01.2.12`) — Annual review of health
provision. "Because the enlargement of the Government Hospital in Haifa was
deferred, the position there became difficult in the intestinal-disease
season." Also: the eastern Galilee short of beds, patients falling back on the
Scottish Mission hospital in Tiberias. **Capacity, and a deferral that the 1935
announcement below finally answers.**

**1933-10-10, Haaretz** (`haretz19331010-01.2.37`) — A passenger riding on the
back of a lorry from Acre to Haifa struck by a passing vehicle, "died shortly
after being brought to the Government Hospital." **Register lead**, early
October 1933.

**1934-01-25, Haaretz** (`haretz19340125-01.2.53.1`) — Department of Health
notice: public auction of a cart and mule at the Health Department office,
"Mountain Road, by the Government Hospital." Fixes the address in the paper's
own words, January 1934.

**1934-03-21, Davar** (`dav19340321-01.2.52`) — Night work at Haifa port; a
full crate falls on a labourer's leg, "the leg was broken, the man taken to the
Government Hospital." **Register lead.**

**1935-09-12, Doar HaYom** (`dhy19350912-01.2.71`) — **The announcement.**
"*Al-Muqattam* reports that the Government intends to build a large government
hospital in Haifa. The surgical department will hold 180 beds and the
department for other diseases 80. The building work will begin shortly and will
take two years." A 260-bed plan, announced September 1935, promised for 1937 —
and the wards were not occupied until October 1938. Read against 1933-07-03,
the deferral and its answer are now both on the record.

**1935-10-21, HaBoker** (`hbkr19351021-01.2.85`) — A feud between two families
at Ein Hod turns into a pitched fight, 37 taking part; "eight of the
combatants were injured and gathered to the Government Hospital in Haifa," two
of them seriously. **Register lead** — a datable multiple admission.

**1935-11-05, HaBoker** (`hbkr19351105-01.2.51`) — The body of the sailor
Muhammad al-Manbi, lost with a Syrian boat and washed ashore near Kfar Samir,
"was taken for examination to the Government Hospital in Haifa." The hospital
as the district mortuary — a function the registers do not record.

**1933-12-17, Haaretz** (`haretz19331217-01.2.23`) `[X]` — The government
hospital at Kerak, Transjordan, sold to the Italian mission. Not Haifa.

**1935-11-19, HaBoker** (`hbkr19351119-01.2.79`) `[X]` — The Northern District

**1935-08-12** (`dav19350812-01.2.43`), **1935-10-02**
(`haretz19351002-01.2.100.1`), **1935-12-26** (`dav19351226-01.2.73`) `[X]` —

## 1936–1938

### The building of Bat Galim, from announcement to ceremony

These articles assemble a continuous chronology of the second hospital. Read
with `[[project_hospital_two_buildings]]` and the NB20→NB21 seam.

**1935-09-12, Doar HaYom** (`dhy19350912-01.2.71`) — the announcement, sourced
to *al-Muqattam*: a large government hospital for Haifa, 180 surgical beds and
80 for other diseases, work to begin shortly and take two years. (See the
1930–35 section.)

**1937-06-17, HaOlam** (`haolam19370617-01.2.10`) — "The Government Department
of Health has signed a contract with *Solel Boneh* for the building of the
Government Hospital in Haifa. The building will cost some ninety thousand
pounds; the installation work has been given to the *Herut* company. Among
other conditions the Government stipulated that Solel Boneh employ 50 per cent
Jews and 50 per cent Arabs. Work will begin in a few days."

**1937-06-18, Davar** (`dav19370618-01.2.93`) — "Work has begun on the building
of the Government Hospital **in Bat Galim**. Some 80 Jewish and Arab workers
are engaged in clearing the plot and in excavation." The first naming of the
site in this corpus.

**1937-07-29, Davar** (`dav19370729-01.2.76`) — The Arab Workers' Society in
Haifa protests the award of the hospital contract to a Jewish contractor,
advising that the Government would do better to carry out the work itself.

**1937-09-05, Davar** (`dav19370905-01.2.129`) — The Arab workers on the site
strike. A delegation to the Deputy District Commissioner for the North
complains that their daily wage is 27–32 grush "while the wage of the Jewish
carpenters comes to 60–75 grush a day." The Deputy Commissioner promises to
equalise the two and to raise the latter. The 50/50 employment clause and what
it was worth in practice, in one paragraph.

**1937-11-11, HaOlam** (`haolam19371111-01.2.6`) — "Work on the building of the
Government Hospital is advancing at a rapid pace. The work, valued at £100,000,
is being done by Solel Boneh. Most of the workers were taken from among the
unemployed. About 100 work daily."

**1938-03-15** (`hbkr19380315-01.2.22`), **1938-05-11** (`hbkr19380511-01.2.71`,
`hzh19380511-01.2.43`) — The Haifa municipal budget and council resolutions
provide £800 "to pave the road leading to the new Government Hospital near Bat
Galim," to be executed by the municipality's own technical department, using
only cement from the *Nesher* works. The approach road is municipal; the
hospital is not.

**1938-04-13, Davar** (`dav19380413-01.2.45`) — Solel Boneh's annual report
lists "the erection of the Government Hospital on the road to Bat Galim" among
its principal works of 1937, alongside Barclays Bank on King's Way.

**1938-08-03, Haaretz** (`haretz19380803-01.2.4`) — A bomb is thrown at a
Jewish lorry "in which several Jewish workers employed on the building of the
Government Hospital by Bat Galim were returning home," at the corner of
Carmel Avenue and Allenby Street in the German Colony. It struck the worker
Isser Tikovsky on the forehead and fell without exploding.

**1938-11-16, Davar and Haaretz** (`dav19381116-01.2.35.2`,
`haretz19381116-01.2.38.1`) — A run of **suppliers' advertisements**: linoleum
and rubber flooring, a carpentry cooperative that "executed all the joinery of
the new Government Hospital building," mirrors and glazing, and Solel Boneh
itself as "the contractor of the new Government Hospital building in Haifa."
The trade ran its congratulations in mid-November, five weeks before the
ceremony.

**1938-11-18, HaBoker** (`hbkr19381118-01.2.28`) — **The load-bearing article.**
A full description, and it is written in the present tense about a working
hospital. In June [1937] the erection of the huge building began, on a site of
60 dunam west of Haifa port; the plot was purchased by the Government for some
£41,000 (its prior designation is unreadable in the OCR). Of the equipment
"transferred from the **old** hospital," over £10,000 more was spent on new
installation. Around the main building stand pavilions for infectious diseases
and others, joined to it by a special roofed passage. "In all the Government
Hospital has for the present **230 beds, among them 90 for infectious
diseases**." And: "of the 110 employees at the Government Hospital there is a
very small percentage of Jewish employees."

> This is dated **five weeks before the opening ceremony** and describes
> equipment already moved and beds already counted. It is independent
> confirmation, from outside the registers, that the hospital was working at
> Bat Galim well before 22 December — consistent with the October 1938
> NB20→NB21 seam and inconsistent with reading the December ceremony as the
> move.

**1938-12-20, Davar** (`dav19381220-01.2.77`) — "On Thursday the official
opening of the new Government Hospital at Bat Galim, Haifa, will take place.
The High Commissioner will be present."

**1938-12-22, Davar** (`dav19381222-01.2.83`) — "Yesterday afternoon the High
Commissioner arrived in Haifa. This morning he will take part in the opening."

**1938-12-23, Davar** (`dav19381223-01.2.66`) — The fullest account of the
ceremony. The building cost **£120,000**. "The plan of the hospital was drawn
by the engineer Mr **A. Mendelsohn**." Five components: (1) a six-storey block
for the beds and the nurses' quarters; (2) a three-storey block for operating
theatres, outpatient reception and laboratory; (3) reception, first aid,
offices and pharmacy; (4) the services wing; (5) the infectious-diseases wing.
Extension is possible and there is ample room for a garden. £100,000 was
allocated at first, raised to £112,000 as materials rose; 200 beds were
provided for, then "24 beds were added for patients from among the military,
and the budget was enlarged again." The High Commissioner's own speech says
**225 beds**, "befitting the needs of this city better than the old hospital
building," and closes: "I declare the hospital officially open, and I wish
that it may remain empty."

**1938-12-23, Haaretz** (`haretz19381223-01.2.5`) — The same ceremony, with two
things Davar does not carry. The institution's **annual budget is put at about
£20,000**. And Dr Kampinsky, Mendelsohn's assistant, speaking in English,
"gave details of the building work and said that **its construction was
completed in September** of this year."

> September finished, October occupied, December opened. The three dates now
> come from three independent directions — the architect's office, the
> registers, and the Government's own ceremony.

**1938-12-23, HaBoker** (`hbkr19381223-01.2.57`) — Mendelsohn was **not at his
own opening**: the Director of Government Medical Services read out the
architect's letter expressing his regret at being absent, having taken leave.
The speakers stress the harmony between Jewish and Arab workers on the site,
and that the building was completed within the required time.

**1938-12-27, Davar, HaBoker, HaZman** (`dav19381227-01.2.16`,
`hbkr19381227-01.2.17`, `hzh19381227-01.2.17`) — The aftermath. The Va'ad
Leumi writes to the Chief Secretary protesting that the speeches of the High
Commissioner, the Director of Medical Services and the Mayor of Haifa "were not
translated into Hebrew," which "gravely offended the feelings of the Jewish
public, which sees in it a contempt for the recognised rights of the Hebrew
language and for the dignity of the Yishuv." Three papers carried it; the
opening was a language incident as much as a medical one.

### The other hospital in the frame

**1938-12-08, Davar** (`dav19381208-01.2.32`) — A polemic on the Jewish
hospital in Haifa: still housed in a private building, without the primitive
conditions a hospital needs, overcrowded beyond all bounds, with no department
of internal medicine at all, and its numbers swollen by three years of
disturbances. "**After the completion of the Government Hospital building at
Bat Galim, the deficiencies of our own hospital will stand out still more.**"
Written a fortnight before the opening. It fixes what the Government Hospital
meant locally: a standard against which the Yishuv measured its own provision
and found it shaming.

**1938-02-25, HaBoker** (`hbkr19380225-01.2.40`) — A civic address noting with
satisfaction that "the new Government Hospital will open shortly," alongside a
new government trades school and the coast road, and complaining that large
areas of unfit housing remain "a danger to health."

## 1939–1941

### A denominator, from the Government's own report

**1940-01-01, HaBoker** (`hbkr19400101-01.2.81`) — A digest of the Department
of Health's **annual report for 1938**, with the average number of beds
occupied daily. Across all government hospitals, 771. Within that:

| institution | avg. beds occupied daily, 1938 |
|---|---:|
| Tel Aviv municipal (counted as governmental for the subsidy) | 290.5 |
| **Government Hospital, Haifa** | **133.5** |
| Nablus | 97.6 |
| Jaffa | 91 |
| Jerusalem | 90.8 |
| Hadassah Jerusalem (voluntary) | 181.2 |
| Beilinson (voluntary) | 102.2 |
| **Hadassah Haifa** (voluntary) | **67.5** |

133.5 beds occupied on an average day in 1938, in a building rated first at 200
and then 224–225. This is directly comparable to the registers and belongs
beside the Blue Book series in `[[project_register_serial_annual_counter]]`.
Note that it straddles the move: 1938 is the last Mountain Road year and the
first Bat Galim months.

**1940-01-10, HaBoker** (`hbkr19400110-01.2.22`) — Government estimates:
£4,913 for furniture and equipment of the Government Hospital in Haifa. The
same column notes that in the mental hospitals Jews were a majority — 83
against 73 Arabs — "a very sad majority."

**1940-01-15, HaMashkif** (`hmf19400115-01.2.41`) — Haifa municipal budget
debate: "in Haifa there is a community hospital, and there is also a government
hospital whose building cost a great deal and whose upkeep too demands much."
The municipality is arguing with the Government over a hospital that is
district, not municipal.

**1941-06-08, HaZman** (`hzh19410608-01.2.28`) — Haifa municipality's 1940
report: the Government has agreed to lease the municipality two stretches of
shore, "one beside the Government Hospital" and one between the electric
company's power station and the mouth of the Kishon. Bat Galim topography, and
a reminder that the new site is a seafront one.

### Named staff

**1939-11-23, HaZman** (`hzh19391123-01.2.46`) — The mayor takes warm leave of
"the director of the Department of Health in Haifa and director of the
Government Hospital, Dr **Mc-Twin** `[?]` (OCR: מק־טווין — McQueen or MacKeown

Safed, doing the district's forensic work, thanked publicly by a patient's
family, and out to a directorship.

### Mass-casualty days — the strongest register leads in the corpus

Each of these should land on an identifiable cluster of admissions.

**1939-06-19/20 — the Haifa market bomb.** Four papers
(`dav19390620-01.2.4`, `dav19390620-01.2.44`, `hbkr19390620-01.2.18`,
`hzh19390620-01.2.12`). Lorries carried "piles of bodies to the Government
Hospital." The dead and injured were partly from Balad al-Sheikh, partly from
Haifa, "but there are also some from Safed and Jerusalem and from various
villages." **The funerals left from the Government Hospital at one in the
afternoon**: fifteen buried in the Muslim cemetery at Balad al-Sheikh, two
taken for burial to Safed, one to Shefa-Amr. And a story the registers cannot
tell: a Jewish pedlar, **Alfred Hahn** (27) — Davar first prints Cohen and
corrects itself in the same issue — went into "the **mortuary** of the
Government Hospital in Haifa, where the victims of the explosion were lying,"
was set upon by Arab women and stabbed in the back; he was moved to Hadassah.
HaBoker has his own account: he had gone to the Government Hospital to get
medical treatment for his own illness, having no money in his pocket, knew
nothing of what had happened in the town, and was attacked in the hospital
yard. He is identified as the *ma'apil* taken with his wife near Migdal.

killed; Ali Masri, Muhammad Hasan and Wadi' al-Shukri wounded, "all transferred

**1940-09-20 — the heavy raid.** (`hbkr19400922-01.2.39`,

**1940-11/12 — the *Patria*.** (`hbkr19401127-01.2.21`, `hmf19401205-01.2.9`)

## 1942–1945

### The isolation ward was *beside* the hospital — a correction to an open question

**1945-07-10, Haaretz** (`haretz19450710-01.2.24`) — A review of the Public
Works Department's wartime building. Under "The war against the plague
epidemic": "The Department of Public Works took part in the operations against
the plague epidemic. **In 1942 the Department set up a special department for
plague sufferers beside the Government Hospital in Haifa**, and in the same
year an isolation pavilion of 14 beds was built for the government hospital at
Beit Safafa near Jerusalem." Further down, among the war years' works, "**to
the Government Hospital in Haifa a pavilion of 20 beds was added**."

**1944-06-01, HaBoker** (`hbkr19440601-01.2.37`) — "Yesterday there died **in
the quarantine house beside the Government Hospital in Haifa** Eliezer ―――, aged
32. It is feared that death came from **smallpox**."

Together with the 1944–45 plague reporting below, which consistently sends
patients to "the isolation unit (מבדדה)", "the department for infectious
diseases **of** the Government Hospital" and "the department for infectious
diseases **beside** the Government Hospital," this bears directly on
`[[project_haifa_lazaret]]`. For plague and smallpox in 1942–45 the isolation
facility was **built by the Government at the hospital and operated as part of
it**, not at a separate lazaret elsewhere in the town. That does not dissolve
the الكرنتينا / المحجر الصحي of the Arabic sources — a port quarantine station
is a different institution with a different job — but it removes the hypothesis
that Haifa's plague and smallpox cases went somewhere the register cannot see.
For these years the register plausibly *is* the denominator. The open question
should be narrowed accordingly rather than left standing.

### Who ran the hospital

**1944-04-16, Yediot Tel Aviv** (`ytlv19440416-01.2.11`) — In a Tel Aviv
municipal debate: "with the appointment of **Dr Mc-Cowen** as director of the
Government Department of Health, in place of **Colonel Heron**, we hope for a
change for the better."

This resolves two earlier readings at once. The man who left the directorship
of the Haifa Government Hospital in November 1939 to become *deputy* director
of the Department of Health (`hzh19391123-01.2.46`, OCR מק־טווין) is the same
Dr McCowen who became its director in 1944 — Haifa was a step on that ladder.
And Colonel Heron is the "Director of Government Medical Services" who read out
Mendelsohn's letter of apology at the December 1938 opening; **the phrase
`לקול הארון` in the Davar report of the ceremony is "to Colonel Heron", not, as
I first read it, a consul.** The correction is recorded here rather than
silently made above.

**1945-04-01, Haaretz** (`haretz19450401-01.2.32`) — "**Dr A. Malchi, director
of the Government Hospital**, has returned from his journey to Egypt on behalf
of the Department of Health and resumed his duties." Malchi runs right through
this corpus: transferred in from Safed in 1931, the district's forensic
physician through the 1930s, thanked in an advertisement by a patient's family
in 1936, out in 1940 to direct the infectious-diseases hospital at Bnei Brak —
and back by 1945 as director of the Haifa hospital itself.

**1945-12-11, Haaretz** (`haretz19451211-01.2.55`) — "**Dr Ibrahim Isa'is** of
Nablus has been appointed **deputy director of the Government Hospital in
Haifa**, in place of **Dr Mishlani**, who has been appointed director of the
Government Hospital in Nablus. Dr Isa'is began work yesterday." Two named Arab
physicians in the senior posts, and the government hospitals working as a
single service with transfers between them.

**1944-04-27, Davar and HaGeh** (`dav19440427-01.2.40`,
`hegeh19440427-01.2.17`) — The Palestine Broadcasting Service schedule: at
8.00 p.m., "a **'By Request' programme specially for the nurses of the
Government Hospital in Haifa**." The nursing staff as a public, named on air.

### What the Yishuv thought the hospital was for

**1942-06-16, HaMashkif** (`hmf19420616-01.2.22`) — Attacking the outgoing
Haifa community council over its neglect of health: the hospitalisation problem
has become acute; a municipal hospital is wanted "especially for people of
small means who, because of their views or their occupation, are not members of
the existing sick funds"; and the Government Hospital — the sentence is damaged
but the sense is that Jewish patients do not turn to it.

**1942-11-05, Kol Israel (Jerusalem)** (`kolisraeljlm19421105-01.2.8`) — Rabbi
Y. Ozer of Haifa on why the new Hadassah building was needed. "The Government
established a magnificent hospital with all the modern improvements, with
departments for every kind of ―――," and then the reason it did not serve:
"**the kitchen of the Government Hospital is absolutely treif**." He is candid
about the limits of protest — one cannot make ultimatums to the authorities
about kashrut — and concludes that "the lack of a kosher Jewish hospital was
felt. The rich used private hospitals. And who suffered? The ordinary Jew."
A religious barrier to use, stated plainly, and worth setting beside the
register's confessional breakdown.

**1944-05-25, HaZman (Tel Aviv)** (`hazmantlv19440525-01.2.20`) — "There exists
in Haifa a large and spacious government hospital, **which the Jewish yishuv
generally has no need of**." By contrast Rothschild is the only place of
hospitalisation for the Jewish sector of Haifa and its immediate district. Its
figures, for comparison with the Government Hospital's 133.5: a surgical
department of 37 beds, a children's department of 13, a maternity department of
7 besides 25 maternity beds moved to the old Hadassah building on Hadar
HaCarmel; a budget of £25,000 and over; a staff of 8 doctors and 54 permanent
employees "including 32 nurses."

**1942-10-29, HaBoker** (`hbkr19421029-01.2.19`) — The ten-year history of
getting Rothschild built, opening on the claim that "**the Jewish hospital
together with the Government Hospital have solved the hospitalisation distress
completely**." PICA gave four dunams on Herzl Street and the Government added
half a dunam of its own adjoining it; the 1934 plan was approved and then
abandoned as too central and too small; ten dunams were bought on the flank of
the Carmel; plans went in in December 1940 and were approved; and "in the
middle of 1941, with the beginning of the bombardment from the air," the
Government approved the plan and issued a licence for scarce building
materials. Engineers Rozov–Woltsch.

**1944-03-01, HaBoker** (`hbkr19440301-01.2.42`) — In the Tel Aviv debate:
"there is a government hospital for Haifa, Jaffa, Nablus and other places, and
only great Tel Aviv has not been granted a government hospital." The Haifa
institution as the thing other cities wanted.

**1942-06-24, HaBoker** (`hbkr19420624-01.2.52`) — A Government tender for
medical stores for the four months August–November 1942. Bids were invited for
"(a) the whole country; (b) the whole country **except Haifa**; (c) **for the
Government Hospital in Haifa alone**." Haifa was large enough to be its own
procurement unit.

announcement of the Government Hospital in Haifa**, those hit by the soldiers'
fire on Sunday evening were the Arab boy Mahmoud Ahmad Muhammad, 13, and the
Arab Rushd Salal Ahmad, 30. The first, who works in a bakery, was hit in the
leg; the second, who happened to be passing in the street, was hit in the neck."

**1947-08-01, HaMashkif** (`hmf19470801-01.2.53`) — "**In an announcement
published yesterday by the Government Hospital in Haifa** it was reported that
Mahmoud Muhammad Shahadi, 21, of Kfar Safa in the Acre sub-district, was shot
near Ramat Yohanan the day before yesterday at 5 p.m. by a Jewish watchman. He
was hit in the forearm of his right hand and his condition is improving."

**1947-02-09** (`ahr19470209-01.2.47`) — "**The Government Hospital in Haifa
reports** four road accidents that occurred today, among them one fatality."

This is methodologically load-bearing and easy to miss. The names, ages, villages
and injuries in these columns are not the reporter's own — they are the
**hospital's own bulletins**, issued to the press. Press names and register
names come from the same clerk. Where they agree, that is not corroboration but
a single source seen twice; where they disagree, the difference is informative.

**And the hospital's names were not always right.** After the June 1946 railway
workshops raid, HaBoker (`hbkr19460619-01.2.9`) notes that "**the CID stuck
names on the bodies, but they think the names are inaccurate.**"

### 17 June 1946 — the railway workshops

Three papers on the same day (`ahr19460619-01.2.9`, `hbkr19460619-01.2.9`,
`hmf19460619-01.2.12`), and between them a whole ward.

**Ten bodies of the attackers and ten wounded** were brought to the Government
Hospital; one of the wounded died the next day. Among the wounded three young
women — **Freda Berlin (24)** and **Malka Granovitch (19)**, both of Tel Aviv,
and **Yehudit Yehezkel (20)** of the Tel Amal quarter in Haifa, who worked at
the Molada hospital. Of the six wounded men two names were known: **Zvi Artal**
and **Gershon Nissim**. A strong guard of soldiers and police was posted beside
the hospital, where nine of the wounded lay.

The two funerals of that day are the sharpest thing in this corpus. Yehiam
Weitz's left Rothschild, and "tens of thousands went up the slope of the
Carmel"; work and trade in Jewish Haifa stopped from eleven. Five hours later
the eleven dead left the Government Hospital's mortuary: "each body covered
with a white sheet, and **on the sole of the left shoe were written the numbers
1 to 11**; so the dead were conveyed by their serial numbers." Behind them
drove a single car of the Hevra Kadisha. "Lonely and forsaken, with no
escorts, the funeral went to the cemetery."

Two follow-ups: **Ida Berlin, 20**, of Tel Aviv, one of the wounded women, left
the Government Hospital on 25 June and was taken to the temporary prison in
Haifa (`hmf19460626-01.2.53`) — note the name and age differ from the 19 June
report, exactly the instability the CID warned of. And **Yitzhak Markovitz**,
one of the eighteen sentenced to life, was brought back from Acre prison to the
Government Hospital for an operation on a shoulder complication and returned
four days later when the doctors decided against operating
(`hbkr19460911-01.2.51`).

Abraham**" — heard "the testimony of **the physician of the Government
Hospital, who examined the bodies of the dead and performed autopsies on
them**," and found both died of army bullets, one to the head, one to the lungs
and heart.

## 1948 — the last five months

### 31 December 1947: the Jewish staff walk out

This is the event `[[project_mb_press_findings]]` reached through the
*Mitteilungsblatt*. The Hebrew dailies carry it in far more detail, including an
official inquiry.

**1948-01-02, Kol HaAm** (`khm19480102-01.2.41`) — "**The Jewish employees left
the Government Hospital.** Haifa, Thursday — **45 Jewish doctors and nurses**
left the Government Hospital yesterday afternoon for want of security, at the
demand of the Yishuv's security committee. The employees did not respond to the
request of the chief physician (an Englishman) to remain, and argued that
nothing had been done to secure their lives and safety there."

**1948-01-05, Al HaMishmar** (`ahr19480105-01.2.59`) — "The strike of **76
Jewish employees** at the Government Hospital here continues, among them **6
doctors and 47 nurses**, declared on Wednesday after their demands for British
guarding and inspection of those entering were not met… **there remain only six
doctors, 16 Arab and Armenian nurses, and six Englishwomen.** After the
management applied to the Red Cross, **27 Arab nurses arrived**, part from
Jerusalem and part volunteers. Meanwhile negotiations continue, but there is
also a proposal to found a new Jewish hospital that would absorb all the
employees."

**1948-01-26, HaMashkif** (`hmf19480126-01.2.34`) — the fullest account, and it
prints the findings of the special commission the Haifa Situation Committee
appointed. Headline: "**The Government Hospital in Haifa has become a purely
Arab institution. The Jews left it because gang members took control of it.**"

The trigger: families of the Jewish patients demanded insistently that they be
moved, and the parents of the pupils of **the hospital's nursing school** feared
for their daughters' lives. "**As a result, on 31.12.47 all the Jewish patients
were transferred out of the Government Hospital, and the medical personnel left
their work.**"

The commission's findings, which are the closest thing this corpus offers to an
inspection of the wards:

- Many employees, "**especially of the lower grades, appeared at the hospital
  armed with firearms**; most also carry knives."
- Threats against the nurses and trainees by low-grade employees and by "**the
  Arab policemen stationed there to guard the prisoners**."
- "The wounded brought to the hospital are accompanied by their relatives and
  friends, or by armed gang members, and often a wounded man has **armed
  bodyguards standing beside him day and night**. These things were done openly
  and unhindered."
- "**In one case two shots were fired inside one of the hospital's lavatories.**"
- "**In one case an Arab prisoner was brought to the hospital and was spirited
  out of it.**"
- "**The hospital is enclosed by a stone wall about a metre and a half high**,"
  which does not answer.
- "Near the turning on the Bat Galim road towards the hospital **the Arab Legion
  is encamped.** There were cases in which Arab Legion sentries threatened Jewish
  nurses and their escorts at gunpoint as they passed, chiefly in the evening —
  **the threat reaching the point of loading a round into the barrel.**"
- "Armed Arabs move about in large groups in the vicinity of the hospital and
  inside its courtyards, and **they penetrate even into the patients' rooms. In
  one case several armed Arabs stopped a nurse and pointed a pistol at her; only
  when it became clear to them that this nurse was an Arab did they let her go.**"

And the settlement that failed: after weeks of negotiation "**the mountain gave
birth to a mouse**" — two gates left open, one British and one Arab policeman at
each, six more British policemen inside; **no Jewish guard**. The Yishuv
institutions judged it insufficient.

> **The single most consequential sentence for this project** sits in that same
> article, as an aside: "**Incidentally: this hospital contains the only
> department for infectious diseases in the north of the country, and that is
> also the only department the Jewish population used.**" The Yishuv asked the
> Government to open an infectious-diseases department inside a Jewish
> settlement, offering to provide the premises if the Government supplied
> equipment and staff. No answer was received.

Two things follow. It sharpens `[[project_haifa_lazaret]]` further — for the
whole north there was no separate infectious-diseases institution, this was it.
And it bears on the confessional composition of the register: Jewish use of the
Government Hospital was, on the Yishuv's own account, largely confined to one
department.

**1948-01-25, Davar** (`dav19480125-01.2.55`) — the same inquiry, angrier, and
with two details HaMashkif omits: the Jewish staff's "pay was meagre" and their
conditions poor, but "**the hospital was made ownerless; the murderous gangs
infused into it a spirit of terror. The health authorities did not heed the
warnings, and when the employees felt their lives to be in danger they left.
Together with them were taken out the few Jewish patients who were there in
those days.**" And, on the day of the refinery riot, a British officer would not
let the hospital's employees leave for Hadar HaCarmel "because of the danger of
travelling by car," and moved them by police car to the nearby quarter.

**1948-01-26, Kol HaAm** (`khm19480126-01.2.57`) — lays the blame on the
Government, "**in light of the fact that a Jewish doctor was murdered within the
precincts of a government hospital (Dr Lehrs at the Government Hospital at Beit
Safafa)**." This identifies the murdered doctor of the January 1948 leader
(`dav19480109-01.2.13`) that I set aside earlier as not-Haifa: it was **Dr
Lehrs at Beit Safafa**, and the exclusion stands.

**1948-01-29, HaBoker** (`hbkr19480129-01.2.90`) — "**The Government Hospital in
Haifa abandoned by the Jews under pressure**," and then a partial return: "**A
late report says: eight Jewish doctors at the Government Hospital in Haifa, and
20 clerks who work in the health department there, returned yesterday to their
work. The nurses and Jewish trainee nurses have not yet returned. A guard of
eight British policemen and two [Arab] policemen has been posted there.**"

So: patients and staff out on 31 December 1947, doctors and clerks back around
28 January 1948, nurses not. Any discontinuity in the ledgers across that seam
now has a documented cause and a date on either side.

### The last director, and the last day

**1948-04-28, four papers** (`haretz19480428-01.2.48`, `hbkr19480428-01.2.44`,
`hmf19480428-01.2.57`, `hzh19480428-01.2.24`) — Six days after the fall of Arab
Haifa, Dr A. Katznelson, director of the Va'ad Leumi's health department, with
his deputy Dr B. Hirshovitz and Dr Y. Meir of the emergency medical committee,
came to Haifa. A district health committee was set up under Dr B. Nissenbaum.
"**In a meeting with Dr McLean, director of the government health office, and Dr
Hamzi, director of the Government Hospital**, Dr Katznelson announced the
institutions' decision **to ensure the orderly continuation of the Government
Hospital for the benefit of the population in general and the Arab population in
particular**, and asked the Arab personnel to continue their work without any
fears. **There is no justification for the abandonment of the institution by the
Arab patients and wounded**, and the Jewish institutions are ready for their
part to give the institution all necessary help."

**Dr Hamzi is Dr N. Hamzeh** — the physician of the Government Hospital who gave
evidence at the trial over the 1 May 1936 disturbances, and who in December 1940
tried to separate the conjoined twins from Shefa-Amr. Twelve years in these
pages, ending as director.

**1948-05-03, Haaretz** (`haretz19480503-01.2.39`) — the closing scene, and the
terminus of the whole register.

> "**The Government Hospital in Haifa — to the municipality.** (From our
> correspondent in Haifa) The Government Hospital in Haifa was transferred
> yesterday to the authority of the Haifa municipality and will serve from now on
> as a municipal hospital. **This hospital contains 288 beds** and is one of the
> largest and finest hospitals in the country. For the present there will be
> opened in it a department for infectious diseases, a surgical department and a
> clinic for the Arab population…
>
> "**Our correspondent, who visited the hospital yesterday, found it completely
> empty.** Apart from the joint guard of Jews and British policemen, a few
> individual British doctors and British nurses, there is not a living soul in
> it. He was told that **immediately upon the conquest of Haifa the Arab
> employees and patients began to leave the hospital in masses. Many patients
> were transferred by ambulance to the environs of Haifa, many others were taken
> by boat to Acre. About 60 Arabs, the last of the patients, were taken out of
> the hospital last Thursday. With them went the last of the nurses, most of them
> Armenian. The Arab doctors also left…**
>
> "Despite his personal promise, **Dr Hamzi's ambulances did not return. Dr Hamzi
> has left for Lebanon.** By contrast, praise is due to the conduct of the
> British chief physician, **Dr MacNeil**, and of the property inspector, **Mr
> Dook (an Arab, a British subject)**, thanks to whom the equipment stores were
> preserved intact.
>
> "**The interior of the hospital arouses nausea.** The great halls, formerly
> gleaming with cleanliness, are filthy and befouled. Here and there stretchers
> still lie stained with blood. **In the mortuary four Arab bodies were still
> lying last night.** The beds are overturned; in the nurses' room there is no
> sign of order. In several departments part of the equipment is missing."

**1948-05-04, six papers** (`ahr19480504-01.2.34`, `ahr19480504-01.2.75`,
`haretz19480504-01.2.42`, `hmf19480504-01.2.48`, `khm19480504-01.2.49`,
`mar19480504-01.2.7`) — the Va'ad Leumi declared the Jewish institutions ready
"**to take upon themselves the care also of the Arab patients and wounded who
are in the hospital**," and put **£10,000** at the municipality's disposal to
ease the transfer. *HaMashkif's* one-liner adds that the transfer was made
"**under the auspices of the International Red Cross**."

**1948-05-07, Haaretz** (`haretz19480507-01.2.59`) — "The mayor of Haifa, Mr
Shabtai Levy, **appealed to all the Arab employees of the Government Hospital**
and to the employees of the land registry department to return to their work,
and said that **he is responsible for their personal safety**."

**1948-05-17, HaZman** (`hzh19480517-01.2.48`) — the government departments in
Haifa pass to the municipality: committees for the port and railways, customs,
fisheries, "**the former Government Hospital**", post and telegraph, lands; and
the Public Works Department hands over its equipment.

The registers begin in 1930 with a hospital on Mountain Road that could not get
its approach paved, and end on 2 May 1948 with 288 beds, four bodies in the
mortuary and a name in the past tense.
